Your relationship to the model

To you the model is a component, usually one you didn't train and can't change. You shape its behavior from the outside with prompts, context, retrieval, tools, and orchestration, and you're responsible for what it does inside your product.

This org covers AI engineering — building with the models: LLM application development, retrieval and RAG, agents and multi-agent systems, evaluation, guardrails, harness engineering, and production operations.

What you own

  • Prompts, context, and retrieval that steer model behavior
  • Tools, agents, and multi-step orchestration
  • Product-level evaluation: does the feature actually work for users?
  • Guardrails and fallbacks in the application layer
  • Choosing which model to call for which job

What you don't own: Training the model, operating the serving fleet, or setting organizational policy. You work inside those constraints and report back when they don't fit the product.

Who you work with

No curriculum owns the model end to end. These are the handoffs with each of the others.

  • AI Workforce

    Work alongside the models

    You hand them
    Tools, agents, and integrations that fit how people actually work, with sensible defaults and guardrails
    They hand you
    Workflow requirements and failure reports from daily use, which tell builders what to fix and what to build next

  • AI Startup

    Turn it into a company

    You hand them
    Working features, honest demos, and a clear picture of what the product can promise customers today
    They hand you
    Prioritized customer problems, what's been promised, and what 'good enough to ship' means for this market

  • AI Governance

    Govern, secure, and assure AI

    You hand them
    System descriptions, eval results, and guardrail designs for each AI feature, plus incident reports when something goes wrong
    They hand you
    Risk tiers, guardrail requirements, and red-team findings to fix before (and after) launch

  • ML Engineering

    Build and train the models

    You hand them
    Real failure cases and task data from production, which tell them where a fine-tune or a different model would actually help
    They hand you
    Models, fine-tunes, and embeddings with documented strengths, failure modes, and benchmark results on relevant tasks

  • AI Infrastructure

    Run the platforms models live on

    You hand them
    Traffic shapes, latency budgets, and model-mix requirements, so capacity and routing match how the product actually calls models
    They hand you
    Serving endpoints with known latency, throughput, rate limits, and cost per call, plus the telemetry to debug them

How the tracks work

Learn

Each track is a sequence of modules: lecture chapters, hands-on exercises, quizzes, and projects. Start with Module 00 and work forward.

Check your work

The paired solutions repo has a reference walkthrough for every exercise. Attempt each one first; the gap between your answer and the reference is where the learning happens.

Built in the open

The content is written with AI assistance and under ongoing human review, so it can contain mistakes. Report anything wrong through the repo's Issues; fixes ship continuously.
